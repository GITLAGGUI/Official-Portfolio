# Data research handoff

Start with the decision the output must support and inspect the actual input files. Record the source, time period, geography, units and collection method for material evidence.

Keep raw inputs separate from cleaned outputs. Make joins, deduplication rules, missing-value treatment and derived fields explicit. Reconcile row counts before and after transformations. A convenience sample is not automatically representative of a population.

For research, distinguish official evidence, third-party interpretation and your own hypothesis. Keep links close to the claims and preserve exact metric scope. Avoid selecting technology before establishing the problem it should solve.

Choose a report structure around the reader's decision. Use tables and visuals where they help compare options or inspect patterns. Keep editable data and formulas available, and verify calculated values against a known sample.

Inspect exported spreadsheets, documents or slides visually before delivery. Provide the usable artifact, supporting data and a concise account of what was checked. Identify limits that could change the conclusion.
